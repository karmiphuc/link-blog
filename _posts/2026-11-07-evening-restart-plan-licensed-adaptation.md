## The habit

The author describes an end-of-day trap: a promising idea makes another twenty minutes of work seem harmless, but unforeseen problems extend it into hours. Instead of trying to finish immediately, spend the remaining time recording the thinking and the steps needed to resume, then stop.

The claim is personal experience, not a guarantee about tomorrow's energy or completion time. The useful part is leaving a concrete re-entry point rather than relying on remembering a moment of enthusiasm.

## An offline restart card

This template is an addition:

```text
Task:
What works now:
Current hypothesis:
Smallest next action:
How I will know that action worked:
Unresolved question or blocker:
Where to resume (file, page, or notebook):
```

Use one line per field when that is enough. Do not turn stopping into a second project. Record the evidence you already have, distinguish a guess from a result, and leave an unfinished experiment explicitly unfinished.

Try the card for several ordinary workdays. On returning, check whether it reduces time spent reconstructing context. If it does not help, simplify the fields or change the stopping point. The aim is an easier return and a protected evening, not a perfect ritual.
