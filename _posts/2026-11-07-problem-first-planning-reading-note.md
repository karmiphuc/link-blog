## The process to remember

Railway describes moving from elaborate solution proposals toward a shared list of problems. Preparing designs before prioritization made people attached to work that might never be selected. Repeating negotiations and resetting the board also consumed time.

Its four-day sequence is:

1. Teams document problems; incomplete entries wait for clarification.
2. Each team assigns tentative priorities, flagging disputed items and dependencies.
3. Teams resolve conflicts and check staffing and existing commitments.
4. They make final commitments and assign a directly responsible individual. Technical proposals follow.

This is the author's account of one company's process, not evidence that the same cadence suits everyone.

## Keep the disagreement

In the discussion, procaryote challenges a fixed quarterly horizon. nradov points out that uncertainty and coordination needs change how far ahead a team can plan. wrs distinguishes committing to investigate a problem from promising a finished outcome. Those cautions matter when adapting the ritual.

## A small experiment

This is a suggested adaptation:

For the next planning session, require one paragraph describing the user's difficulty before accepting a feature proposal. Add an owner, the evidence available, unresolved dependencies and a date to revisit assumptions. Separate a commitment to explore from a commitment to deliver. At the review, count decisions clarified and surprises discovered, rather than celebrating a completed planning document.
