## Keep the useful parts

Goel proposes reducing uncertainty before an event: know why you are attending, the setting, arrival plan and journey home. During conversation, listen for a specific interest and ask about it instead of rushing to deliver your own story. A concrete follow-up related to the conversation can be more useful than a generic greeting.

These are the author's personal suggestions, not established rules about every introvert. The opening discussion challenges the framing: needing recovery time and finding an interaction distressing are different experiences. A rigid performance checklist may add friction for some people. This note does not preserve the entire discussion or treat its anecdotes as clinical advice.

## A lighter version to carry

This is an adaptation, not a script from the source:

- Before leaving, choose one reason to attend and one acceptable way to leave. There is no requirement to meet everyone.
- Start with a genuine question about something shared in the setting. Let the answer determine the next question.
- If a group or environment is a poor fit, change the setting or take a break rather than treating it as a personal failure.
- Afterward, note only what is useful and appropriate to remember. If a follow-up would benefit both people, make it specific and optional.

A conversation is not a conversion funnel. Keep the preparation that lowers effort and discard the choreography that makes you feel less present.
